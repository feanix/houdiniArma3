Reference / Nodes

Weight Transfer

Copies the bone weights of a source body (the sample character) onto your mesh, with layers for rigid and loose parts.

Copies the bone weights of a source body (for example Bohemia's sample character) onto your mesh. Each point of your mesh takes the weights of the closest point on the body when it is near enough and faces the same way. The points that don't match are filled in smoothly from their neighbours.

Three groups let you treat parts of the mesh differently. Rigid parts (a helmet) get one bone or one fixed blend per piece. Loose parts (a bandolier) take the weights under them and are smoothed along the piece. Everything else is the first pass. A point string a3ob_rig_bone on a rigid piece names its bone. The result is bone selections in the model.cfg spelling, with a3ob_rig_origin (where each weight came from) and a3ob_rig_piece. Run Weight Cleanup after it.

Houdini namea3ob_weight_transfer
Where to find itTab menu, A3OB, Weight Transfer
Inputs3

Inputs

  1. Input 1: Your mesh. The mesh that gets the weights. It should lie on top of the source body (fit it first).
  2. Input 2: Source body. The body with bone weights: bone selections (point groups with a3ob_w weights), for example the sample character from Extract LOD. The output of Rig Fit goes here as it is: its fitted sample body is used.
  3. Input 3: Skeleton. The skeleton, for example from Rig Skeleton. Its joint names decide which selections are bones and how they are spelled; its hierarchy says where a twist bone's weight goes.

Parameters

Parameters are in the order they appear on the node.

First Pass firstpass

The base mesh (for example the uniform). It gets its weights straight from the source body. Empty means every point that is in neither of the two groups below.

Type
Group picker (text; the menu lists the point groups of the input, the arrow button picks in the viewport)
Default
(empty)

Rigid Parts rigid

Parts that must not stretch (a helmet, a pouch). Each connected piece gets one bone, or one fixed blend, from the first-pass points it sits on.

Type
Group picker (text; the menu lists the point groups of the input, the arrow button picks in the viewport)
Default
(empty)

Loose Parts nonrigid

Parts that bend (a bandolier, a cable). Each point takes the weights of the first pass under it, and the weights are smoothed along the piece.

Type
Group picker (text; the menu lists the point groups of the input, the arrow button picks in the viewport)
Default
(empty)

Match Distance distance

How far from the source body a point may be and still copy its weights, as a share of the size of your mesh (the diagonal of its bounding box). Points that are farther are filled in from their neighbours.

Type
Number
Default
0.05
Allowed
At least 0; no upper limit

Match Angle angle

How much the surface direction may differ, in degrees, for a point to copy weights. A point that faces another way (a sleeve against the torso) is filled in instead.

Type
Number
Default
35
Allowed
From 0 to 180

Rigid Parts Use rigid_mode

How a rigid piece is weighted.

Type
Menu
Default
Dominant bone

Choices:

  • Dominant bone dominant: The whole piece follows the one bone with the most weight under it (weight 1).
  • Fixed blend blend: Every point gets the same mix of bones, the average under the piece.

Merge Twist Bones merge_twist

For a rigid piece, the weight of a twist bone (a name ending in Roll) counts for the next bone down the chain that is not a twist, so a piece on the elbow follows the forearm. Only used with Dominant bone.

Type
On or off
Default
On

Smoothing Passes smooth

How many times the weights of a loose part are averaged with their neighbours along the piece.

Type
Whole number
Default
8
Allowed
From 0 to 100

Smallest Piece minpiece

A piece with fewer points than this is ignored: it gets no weights from the rigid or loose layer. Loose pieces of the source body this small (the sample's proxy triangles) are not used either.

Type
Whole number
Default
10
Allowed
At least 1; no upper limit
